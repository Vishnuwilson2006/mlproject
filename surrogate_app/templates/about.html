ECE Final Year Major Project

Machine Learning-Based Surrogate Model for Circuit Performance Prediction

Replacing differential equation SPICE solvers with high-speed Random Forest regression models.

Traditional SPICE Bottleneck

In analog integrated circuit (IC) design, iterative design space exploration requires running thousands of transient and AC SPICE simulations (LTspice, HSPICE, Spectre). Each simulation solves non-linear differential equations, taking seconds to minutes per iteration, drastically slowing down automatic sizing and optimization routines.

ML Surrogate Solution

A surrogate model acts as an approximation function $f(X) \approx Y$. Once trained on a comprehensive synthetic physics dataset, the Random Forest model maps component values ($R_1, R_2, R_C, R_E, C_1, C_2$) directly to performance outputs ($\text{Gain}, f_c, \phi_m$) in sub-millisecond execution times.

Underpinning Physics & Circuit Equations

1. Voltage Gain ($A_v$)

$$A_v = 20 \log_{10} \left| \frac{R_C}{r_e + R_E} \right|$$ Small-signal emitter resistance $r_e = \frac{V_T}{I_E}$ where thermal voltage $V_T \approx 26\text{mV}$.

2. Cutoff Frequency ($f_c$)

$$f_c \approx \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot (R_{eq1} C_1 + R_C C_2)}$$ Determined by low-frequency pole locations established by coupling capacitors $C_1$ and $C_2$.

3. Phase Margin ($\phi_m$)

$$\phi_m = 180^\circ - \sum \arctan\left(\frac{f}{f_p}\right)$$ Reflects dynamic frequency response stability across multi-pole RC networks.
